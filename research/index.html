Research

My research interests center on demography, administrative data, and women's health. I am particularly drawn to understanding how demographic and health-related characteristics influence health outcomes and service utilization, and how quantitative evidence can inform public health policy, programs, and interventions.

My current research spans maternal health and community-based service evaluation. Using CDC National Vital Statistics System (NVSS) data, I am examining racial disparities within cesarean delivery and labor induction, as well as differences in trial of labor after cesarean (TOLAC) usage and success across racial and regional groups. Through the UW capstone program, I am also contributing to an evaluation of the Downtown Emergency Services Center's (DESC) STAR Center. This has involved examining program processes and effectiveness through measures such as demographic participation, utilization of within-center services, and crisis care interventions. Across these projects, I have contributed to statistical analysis planning, data analysis, and communication and interpretation of results.

My substantative research projects are included below with synopses. Software-focused and/or smaller projects are included on this site under software.

Racial Disparities in Delivery Methods (March 2026 - Present)

I have been working with Dr. Ellen Kauffman and Dr. Kathleen Kerr on racial disparities in delivery methods using the CDC NVSS natality microdata.

Our work examines racial disparities in cesarean and induction rates within an NTSV population, including evaluation of these disparities across differing levels of comorbidity burden.

By stratifying by race and comorbidity burden, we aim to evaluate the robustness of observed racial disparities in the context of maternal health characteristics. Considering induction rates alongside cesarean rates may also help identify potential avenues for clinical intervention.

While this research is in the submission phase, we are also investigating trial of labor after cesarean (TOLAC) success rates across racial and regional groups. This additional work uses the CDC NVSS aggregate dataset due to restrictions on regional variables in the public-use microdata.

Evaluation of DESC's STAR Center (October 2026 - Present)

Through the UW capstone program, I have begun a project with the Downtown Emergency Services Center (DESC) to conduct an evaluation of their new Stability Through Access and Resources (STAR) Center. This project involves analyzing the processes and effectiveness of the STAR Center as a whole using various metrics relating to demographic participation, use of within-center services, and more. This work has also been in direct collaboration with two MS student colleagues alongside DESC research staff.

Recent work has involved the creation of a statistical analysis plan (SAP) to facilitate future work. This has involved nuanced descriptions of how to turn objectives into actionable analyses. Sections of the SAP relate to modeling methods, derived variables, planned data displays, and missing data handling.

Additional effort has gone into creating a project management plan (PMP) for even distribution of workload between members, while also focusing on utilizing the specific skills of each individual. Beyond this, I have acted as the main point of contact over email between DESC and the MS student team; this has involved coordinating meeting times, exchanging information, and ensuring that all parties are on the same page.

Independent Project: Effectiveness of ChatGPT's Poetry (2023 - 2024)

At the University of Illinois at Springfield (UIS), I worked on research relating to AI's capabilities in generating form-based poetry under Dr. Hei-Chi Chan. I presented to the UIS STARS Symposium and received the Best Presentation in Humanities award, and spoke in an interview on this research for the UIS AI Campus Learning Community.

This project was based in qualitative data, referencing both statistical methods alongside linguistic and literary guidelines. Poetry was restricted to traditional forms (e.g. Shakespearean sonnet) so that the texts could be more clearly defined, and defined poetic measures of success included adherence to form, readability/grammar, focus on a (few) central idea(s), and novelty. I fully developed measures for adherence to form and readability/grammar, and was in the progress of working with measures for novelty prior to time-related constraints.
